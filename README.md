<h1 style="text-align: justify;">How to Prepare for The Open Group OG0-093 Exam</h1>

<p style="text-align: justify;">Enterprise architecture helps organizations connect their business goals with technology decisions. The Open Group OG0-093 exam tests whether you understand how to plan, develop, and manage enterprise architecture using the TOGAF 9 framework.</p>

<p style="text-align: justify;">This exam combines two levels of knowledge. You need to understand TOGAF concepts, but you must also know how to apply them when an organization faces real business and technology challenges.</p>

<h2 style="text-align: justify;">What Does the OG0-093 Exam Cover?</h2>

<p style="text-align: justify;">OG0-093 is the TOGAF 9 Combined Part 1 and Part 2 exam. It covers the TOGAF Standard, Version 9.2.</p>

<p style="text-align: justify;">You should understand how enterprise architecture supports business strategy, improves communication between teams, and guides technology investments.</p>

<p style="text-align: justify;">For example, imagine a company wants to move several business applications to the cloud. Before choosing a cloud platform, an enterprise architect needs to understand business requirements, existing applications, data dependencies, security needs, and future goals. TOGAF provides a structured approach to managing this work.</p>

<h2 style="text-align: justify;">How Does the Architecture Development Method Work?</h2>

<p style="text-align: justify;">The Architecture Development Method (ADM) is one of the most important areas to study. It provides a step-by-step approach to developing and managing enterprise architecture.</p>

<p style="text-align: justify;">Understand the purpose of each phase:</p>

<ul>
	<li>
	<p style="text-align: justify;"><strong>Preliminary Phase:</strong> Prepare the organization and establish its architecture approach.</p>
	</li>
	<li>
	<p style="text-align: justify;"><strong>Phase A &ndash; Architecture Vision:</strong> Define the project scope, business goals, and high-level direction.</p>
	</li>
	<li>
	<p style="text-align: justify;"><strong>Phase B &ndash; Business Architecture:</strong> Describe business processes, capabilities, and organizational needs.</p>
	</li>
	<li>
	<p style="text-align: justify;"><strong>Phase C &ndash; Information Systems Architectures:</strong> Develop data and application architecture.</p>
	</li>
	<li>
	<p style="text-align: justify;"><strong>Phase D &ndash; Technology Architecture:</strong> Define the technology infrastructure needed to support the solution.</p>
	</li>
	<li>
	<p style="text-align: justify;"><strong>Phase E &ndash; Opportunities and Solutions:</strong> Identify possible solutions and implementation options.</p>
	</li>
	<li>
	<p style="text-align: justify;"><strong>Phase F &ndash; Migration Planning:</strong> Create a practical roadmap for moving from the current state to the target state.</p>
	</li>
	<li>
	<p style="text-align: justify;"><strong>Phase G &ndash; Implementation Governance:</strong> Check that implementation projects follow the approved architecture.</p>
	</li>
	<li>
	<p style="text-align: justify;"><strong>Phase H &ndash; Architecture Change Management:</strong> Manage changes after the architecture has been implemented.</p>
	</li>
</ul>

<p style="text-align: justify;">Requirements Management continues throughout the ADM cycle.</p>

<p style="text-align: justify;">Do not just memorize the phase names. Learn what each phase produces and when an architect should use it. For example, if a project team is building a solution that does not follow approved architecture standards, Implementation Governance is the relevant area.</p>

<h2 style="text-align: justify;">How Do Architecture Building Blocks and the Repository Work?</h2>

<p style="text-align: justify;">TOGAF uses Architecture Building Blocks (ABBs) and Solution Building Blocks (SBBs) to describe architecture components.</p>

<p style="text-align: justify;">An ABB represents a required capability or architectural concept. An SBB describes a specific solution that can deliver that capability.</p>

<p style="text-align: justify;">For example, an organization may need a secure identity management capability. That requirement can be described as an architecture building block. A selected identity management platform can then serve as part of the solution.</p>

<p style="text-align: justify;">Also study the Architecture Repository. It stores architecture information, including reference materials, standards, models, and reusable building blocks. Understand the difference between the Architecture Repository, Architecture Landscape, Standards Information Base, and Reference Library.</p>

<h2 style="text-align: justify;">Why Are Business, Data, Application, and Technology Architectures Important?</h2>

<p style="text-align: justify;">These four areas help architects understand an organization from different perspectives.</p>

<ul>
	<li>
	<p style="text-align: justify;"><strong>Business Architecture:</strong> Explains business strategy, processes, structure, and capabilities.</p>
	</li>
	<li>
	<p style="text-align: justify;"><strong>Data Architecture:</strong> Defines how business data is organized, stored, managed, and accessed.</p>
	</li>
	<li>
	<p style="text-align: justify;"><strong>Application Architecture:</strong> Describes applications and how they interact with each other.</p>
	</li>
	<li>
	<p style="text-align: justify;"><strong>Technology Architecture:</strong> Covers the infrastructure, platforms, and technical services that support applications and data.</p>
	</li>
</ul>

<p style="text-align: justify;">Consider a retail company introducing an online ordering system. Business Architecture defines the ordering process. Data Architecture covers customer and product information. Application Architecture explains how the website connects with inventory and payment systems. Technology Architecture defines the servers, networks, and platforms supporting these services.</p>

<p style="text-align: justify;">Understanding these connections helps you answer scenario-based questions.</p>

<h2 style="text-align: justify;">How Should You Approach Architecture Governance and Migration Planning?</h2>

<p style="text-align: justify;">Architecture governance ensures that projects follow agreed principles, standards, and decisions. It helps prevent individual teams from building solutions that create security risks, duplicate systems, or integration problems.</p>

<p style="text-align: justify;">Migration planning focuses on moving from the current architecture to the target architecture. Architects may need to identify dependencies, prioritize projects, estimate costs, and plan implementation stages.</p>

<p style="text-align: justify;">For example, a company may not be able to replace every legacy application at once. The architect must identify which systems should move first, which applications depend on them, and how business operations can continue during the transition.</p>

<h2 style="text-align: justify;">How Can You Prepare for Scenario-Based Questions?</h2>

<p style="text-align: justify;">The second section requires more than memorizing definitions. You need to understand the situation described and choose the most suitable TOGAF approach.</p>

<p style="text-align: justify;">Try this example:</p>

<p style="text-align: justify;">A company is planning to replace an outdated application platform. Different departments have different requirements, and the project team wants to begin implementation immediately.</p>

<p style="text-align: justify;">Before selecting a solution, an architect should understand stakeholder concerns, define the Architecture Vision, review business and technical requirements, and assess the available options.</p>

<p style="text-align: justify;">When practising similar scenarios, ask yourself:</p>

<ul>
	<li>
	<p style="text-align: justify;">What is the main business problem?</p>
	</li>
	<li>
	<p style="text-align: justify;">Which ADM phase is relevant?</p>
	</li>
	<li>
	<p style="text-align: justify;">What architecture information is needed?</p>
	</li>
	<li>
	<p style="text-align: justify;">Which stakeholders should be involved?</p>
	</li>
	<li>
	<p style="text-align: justify;">What governance or migration activity is required?</p>
	</li>
</ul>

<p style="text-align: justify;">This approach helps you understand why an answer is correct instead of relying on memorized choices.</p>
